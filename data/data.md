## 3. Elderly Care & Medical Knowledge Base

### 1. Three-Meal Nutrition Guide for Older Adults
* **General Principles**: Eat regular meals at consistent times and portions, keep food mild and lower in salt, include variety, prioritize easy chewing and digestion, and maintain adequate fluid intake.
* **Breakfast**: Include quality protein and complex carbohydrates—such as oatmeal or soft rice, eggs/milk/soy milk, and a small portion of vegetables—to support steady morning energy.
* **Lunch**: Serve as a fuller nutrient-dense meal with moderate lean meat, fish/shrimp, or tofu, plenty of vegetables, and mixed grains or soft rice without excessive grease.
* **Dinner**: Keep it lighter, moderate in portion, and relatively early—focusing on easily digestible vegetables, legumes, fish/poultry, or congee/noodles—while limiting late-night high-sugar, high-fat, or overly salty foods.
* **Hydration & Snacks**: Sip water gradually throughout the day rather than waiting until feeling thirsty; for between-meal snacks, plain yogurt, small portions of low-sugar fruit, or whole-grain crackers work well.

### 2. Common Chronic Conditions in Older Adults

#### (1) Type 2 Diabetes
* **Overview**: A common metabolic condition in older adults associated with insufficient insulin secretion or reduced insulin sensitivity; persistently elevated blood sugar can affect blood vessels, nerves, eyes, and kidneys over time.
* **Care Focus**: Monitor blood glucose regularly, take prescribed medications as directed, maintain consistent meals and light physical activity, and watch for hypoglycemia signs (cold sweat, tremors, dizziness, confusion) as well as foot skin injuries.

#### (2) Hypertension
* **Overview**: Persistently elevated arterial blood pressure is common in older adults and may cause no obvious symptoms early on, while long-term poor control increases strain on the heart, brain, and blood vessels.
* **Care Focus**: Reduce sodium from cooking and processed foods, maintain steady routines and calm mood, and rise slowly from sitting or lying positions to reduce orthostatic dizziness and fall risk.

#### (3) Osteoporosis
* **Overview**: Loss of bone mass and deterioration of bone microarchitecture lead to more fragile bones and higher fracture risk in older adults, where minor bumps or falls can cause fractures.
* **Care Focus**: Support adequate dietary calcium and vitamin D along with appropriate weight-bearing or balance exercises; keep home walkways dry, well-lit, and clear of tripping hazards to prevent falls.

## 4. Everyday Recipes

### 1. Tomato Beef Brisket Stew (Pairs Well with Soft Rice or Mixed Grain Rice)
* **Ingredients**: 400g beef brisket, 3 tomatoes, 1/2 onion, 3 slices ginger, a few scallion segments, 1 tbsp vegetable oil, 1 tbsp light soy sauce, a pinch of salt, water as needed.
* **Instructions**:
  1. Cut the beef brisket into cubes, blanch in cold water to skim foam, and drain; peel and chop the tomatoes, and slice the onion.
  2. Heat a little oil in a pot, sauté the ginger and onion until fragrant, then add the tomatoes and stir until saucy.
  3. Add the beef and toss well, pour in light soy sauce and enough warm water to cover, bring to a boil, then simmer gently for 60–75 minutes until tender.
  4. Season lightly with salt near the end if needed and top with scallion segments before serving.

### 2. Steamed Sea Bass with Broccoli
* **Ingredients**: 1 whole sea bass (approx. 450g), 150g broccoli, julienned ginger, julienned scallions, 1.5 tbsp steamed fish soy sauce, 1 tbsp vegetable oil.
* **Instructions**:
  1. Clean the fish, score the sides lightly, and place part of the ginger on top; cut the broccoli into small florets and wash.
  2. Once water in the steamer boils, steam the fish over high heat for 8–10 minutes until cooked through, then pour off excess liquid and discard the used ginger.
  3. Blanch the broccoli in boiling water and arrange on a plate; top the fish with fresh ginger and scallions, drizzle with steamed fish soy sauce, and pour 1 tbsp hot oil over the aromatics.

### 3. Silky Steamed Egg with Shrimp
* **Ingredients**: 2 eggs, 6–8 peeled shrimp, ~120ml warm water (about 1.5x egg volume), 1 tsp light soy sauce, a few drops sesame oil, chopped scallions.
* **Instructions**:
  1. Beat the eggs with warm water and a tiny pinch of salt, skimming off surface bubbles; toss the shrimp with a little cooking wine and white pepper for 5 minutes.
  2. Pour the egg mixture into a heatproof bowl, cover loosely with heat-safe wrap or a small plate, and steam over medium-low heat for about 6 minutes until half-set.
  3. Add the shrimp and steam for another 3–4 minutes until the shrimp turn opaque and the egg custard is fully set.
  4. Drizzle with light soy sauce and a few drops of sesame oil, then garnish with chopped scallions.
